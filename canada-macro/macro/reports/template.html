Construction notes

  • BC real GDP is published annually only (table 36-10-0711). The monthly series used here is a Chow-Lin disaggregation (AR(1) residual, ρ = {{ "%.2f"|format(chow_lin.rho) }}). Its indicators are Canada monthly real GDP and BC LFS employment. It matches published annual totals through {{ chow_lin.benchmarked_until.year }}; later months are indicator-based extrapolations.
  • Population (quarterly, a stock on the first day of the quarter) is interpolated to monthly with a PCHIP spline on log levels and flagged. Monthly population growth is therefore smooth within each quarter.
  • Policy rate: Valet's V39079 starts in April 2009. Earlier months use the Bank Rate (V122530) minus 25bp, which equals the overnight target since 1996 by construction of the operating band. On the overlap the median spread is {{ "%.3f"|format(splice.median_spread) }} pp, and {{ "%.0f"|format(100*splice.share_exact) }}% of months are exactly 25bp. The rest are rate-decision months, where a point-in-time value is compared with a monthly average.

Average weekly earnings use the seasonally adjusted table 14-10-0223 rather than the unadjusted 14-10-0203. 2. Stationarity report (BVAR variables)

ADF (H0: unit root) and KPSS (H0: stationary) tests at 5%. A series counts as stationary only if ADF rejects and KPSS does not. Otherwise it is log-differenced (×100, % m/m), or first-differenced for rates.

{{ stationarity_table|safe }}

Monthly BC CPI inflation rejects a unit root (ADF) but KPSS also rejects stationarity at 5%. This is consistent with a level shift in inflation in 2021–23. It is modelled once-differenced, the standard choice, and flagged here.

5. Nowcast: current-quarter BC real GDP growth

Mixed-frequency dynamic factor model (statsmodels DynamicFactorMQ): one factor with AR(2) dynamics and AR(1) idiosyncratic errors. Monthly indicators are in growth rates; the quarterly target is q/q growth of Chow-Lin BC GDP, observed through {{ nowcast.last_target_q }}. The Kalman filter handles the ragged edge and the short retail and permit histories.

7. Caveats

  • New homes only. The NHPI measures contractors' selling prices of new houses of unchanged quality: single, semi-detached and row houses (no condominium apartments). It does not cover resale homes, which dominate Vancouver transactions, and it is not a measure of the broader housing market.
  • Not a property valuation. This is a trend and scenario tool for regional aggregates. It says nothing about the value of any individual property.
  • Revisions. GDP, LFS (annual revisions and periodic rebasing), SEPH earnings, retail sales and population estimates are all revised. The backtests use today's vintage, so they understate real-time forecast errors. STL seasonal factors and the Chow-Lin estimate are fitted on the full sample, so they also use information that would not have been available at each origin.
  • Modelled BC GDP. StatCan publishes no monthly or quarterly BC real GDP. The monthly series and the nowcast target come from Chow-Lin and are only as good as the link between BC output and its indicators (Canada GDP, BC employment). Months after {{ chow_lin.benchmarked_until.year }} are not benchmarked.
  • Structural breaks. The 2020 pandemic (huge, short-lived swings; Mar–Aug 2020 excluded from BVAR estimation) and the 2022–23 tightening (the fastest since the 1990s, after a long near-zero period) break the linear, constant-parameter assumptions. 2009–15 and 2020–21 sat at or near the effective lower bound, where rate shocks are not well defined. The housing relation changes sharply between samples.
  • Identification. Cholesky IRFs depend on the variable ordering and show standard puzzles (activity rising after tightening). They are scenario inputs, not causal estimates.
  • No cointegration in the housing module. The long-run elasticities come from a levels regression that fails a cointegration test. They describe co-movement in this sample, not a stable demand relation.
  • Interpolated data. Population growth is interpolated from quarterly estimates, so its monthly dynamics are artificial.
  • Bands. Forecast bands reflect parameter and shock uncertainty within the model, not model uncertainty, data revisions or breaks. Treat them as lower bounds on true uncertainty.
